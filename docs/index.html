01 / THE QUESTIONWHY THIS STUDY

Better efficacy is only
part of the story.

Obesity care needs treatment that people can access, tolerate, and continue.

GLP-1/GIP therapies changed expectations for weight loss. In routine care, however, treatment trajectories vary. This study asks how safety signals look when placed in the context of observed treatment duration.

THE NEED
1 in 8

People worldwide lived with obesity in 2022.

A chronic condition that often requires sustained care.

WHO, Obesity and overweight (2025)
THE THERAPEUTIC SHIFT
15–21%

Mean weight reduction in pivotal trials.

Separate semaglutide and tirzepatide trials; not a head-to-head comparison.

Wilding et al., NEJM (2021); Jastreboff et al., NEJM (2022)
THE ROUTINE-CARE GAP
54.9%

Discontinued within one year.

One health-system cohort of 8,184 initiators. Stopping has multiple causes.

Gasoyan et al., Obesity (2025)
More clinical context+

A continuum of care

Nutrition, physical activity, behavioral support, pharmacotherapy, and metabolic or bariatric procedures can contribute to treatment. Patient response, access, and persistence differ.

Tolerability matters

Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, and abdominal discomfort can affect continuation. Other safety concerns include pancreatitis, gallbladder events, and heart-rate changes; labeling varies by drug.

Discontinuation is multifactorial

Among 288 chart-reviewed discontinuers in the cited study, 14.6% stopped because of intolerable side effects. This uses a different denominator from the 54.9% figure.

Presentation slides 3–6. WHO; NIDDK treatment guidance; FDA prescribing information; Gasoyan et al. (2025), as cited in the supplied slides.

Study design, source roles & cohort definitions+

Different sources, different questions

Trials offer structured follow-up and stronger attribution. FAERS prioritizes reporting patterns without a population exposure denominator. EHR data add clinical context but retain confounding and incomplete capture.

Medication scope

The targets are liraglutide, semaglutide, and tirzepatide. The broader baseline includes core AOMs, metabolic medications, and background treatments. These do not all share the same indication or regulatory status for obesity treatment.

The analytic EHR cohort

The supplied diagram starts with 365,091 obesity and 260,987 overweight patients. After the stated exclusions, 610,372 remain; 71,316 have AOM exposure.

Presentation slides 7–14 and 21; manuscript Methods and Tables 1–2. The sources are not linked at the individual-patient level.

How to read these counts+

Completed duration

The x-axis groups episodes by completed treatment duration. It does not show when an event occurred within the same patients' follow-up.

Prior recorded diagnoses

The study applies a phenotype-specific 365-day lookback before episode start. An absent prior code is not proof of absent prior disease.

Denominator definitions

The text reports 28,961 filtered GLP-1/GIP records, while Figure 5 column Ns sum to 33,647. This demo preserves source counts and does not derive new rates from these denominators.

04 / INTERPRETATIONWHAT THE PATTERNS CAN—AND CANNOT—TELL US

One pattern.
Several explanations.

A lower association in longer-duration groups does not, by itself, tell us why it is lower.

These are competing hypotheses, not mechanisms established by the study. The hepatic finding does not establish drug-induced liver disease.

Where uncertainty remains+

Reporting & normalizationUnderreporting, stimulated reporting, and classification errors affect the global screen.

Semantic granularitySymptoms and coded diagnoses are not one-to-one; mappings can exclude or consolidate concepts.

Signal scopeThe shared intersection omits drug-specific signals.

Clinical captureOne health system and out-of-network care limit generalizability.

Residual confoundingIndication, concurrent treatments, dose, and selection complicate causal interpretation.
